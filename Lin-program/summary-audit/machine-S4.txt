

4. 
Proofs





In this section we explain the structure and notations in our machine generated proofs. We call it the Table of Proofs. The Table of Proofs is provided in two different forms in the Zenodo repo: a single sqlite3 database file 
proofs.db
 and 22 plain-text tables 
proofs-part1.csv
–
proofs-part22.csv
. The plain-text table is divided into 22 table files because there are more than 21 million rows in the table and some table viewers like Microsoft Excel has a row limit of one million. We encourage the reader to use the open source software DB Browser for SQLite to access the table in 
proofs.db
. This tool can help you quickly search for what you want in big tables. You can also execute advanced SQL queries in the software.

The first column 
id
 refers to the line number in the table. The columns 
depth
 and 
reason
 refer to the structure of the proof which we will explain in more details later. The column 
name
 refers to the name of a CW spectra or a map in a cofiber sequence.



 •
 



If 
name
 is the name of a CW spectrum like the first row, then the columns 
stem,s,r,x,dx
 refer to an Adams differential of the spectrum. In our example, this is










$d_{3}([0,2])=[0,1]$







for










$[0,2]\in E_{2}^{16,107+16}(\verb|Ceta|)$







where 
$[0,2]$
 denotes the linear combination of the 0th and 2nd basis elements in 
$E_{2}^{16,107+16}$
 in the basis table of 
Ceta
 and 
$[0,1]$
 denotes the linear combination in the bidegree that this differential hits.

 
 •
 



If 
name
 is the name of a map in a cofiber sequence, it is of the form 
X__Y__Z:n
 where










$\verb|X|\xrightarrow{f}\Sigma^{?}\verb|Y|\xrightarrow{g}\Sigma^{?}\verb|Z|\xrightarrow{h}\Sigma\verb|X|$







is a cofiber sequence and 
$n=0,1,2$
 correspond to 
$f,g,h$
 respectively. The columns 
stem,s,r,x,dx
 refer to an extension of the map. In our example (second row of Table 
17
), consider the cofiber sequence










$\verb|Cnu|\xrightarrow{f}\verb|CW_nu_eta_2|\xrightarrow{g}\Sigma^{6}\verb|C2|\xrightarrow{h}\Sigma\verb|Cnu|.$







The second row of the table indicates that there is a 
$g$
-extension from the permanent cycle










$[0]\in E_{2}^{2,15+2}(\verb|CW_nu_eta_2|)$







to the permanent cycle










$[0]\in E_{2}^{4,15+4-6}(\verb|C2|)$







which jumps filtration by 
$r=2$
.

 







Next we explain the structure of the Table of Proofs. A typical proof of an Adams differential has the following form.






Proof Structure 4.1
.




Assume that the value of an Adams differential 
$d_{r}(x)$
 has four candidates 
$y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}$
. (Usually the number of candidates is a power of two.)



 •
 



If 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{1}$
, propagate (by the Leibniz rule, naturality, …). Get a contradiction.

 
 •
 



If 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{2}$
, propagate. Get a contradiction.

 
 •
 



If 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{3}$
, propagate. Get a contradiction.

 



Then we can conclude that 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{4}$
.

Here the 
depth
 column can be thought as the nesting level of the latex 
itemize
 environment. Rows with 
reason=`T'
 correspond to assumptions (‘T’ is a symbol for ‘Try’). Each assumption row has a multi-line proof in the 
info
 column which explains why the assumption leads to a contradiction. For example, the actual content of 
proof1
 is the following.





 
Get Csigma (116,10) d_3[1]=[1,2]. Apply the Leibniz rule with
S0 (0,1) d_3[0]=[] and get Csigma (116,11) d_3[]=[1,2].
However, Csigma (115,14) [1,2] is not in B_2.
 







In 
proof1
 the element 
Csigma (115,14) [1,2]
 refers to the element










$[1,2]\in E_{2}^{14,115+14}(\verb|Csigma|).$







Rows with 
reason=`D'
 correspond to the conclusions after we rule out enough candidates (‘T’ rows) of the value of Adams differentials or extensions (‘D’ is a symbol for ‘Deduction’).

Next we give examples of more complex proofs of Adams differentials.






Proof Structure 4.2
.




Assume that the value of an Adams differential 
$d_{r}(x)$
 has four candidates 
$0,y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}$
.



 •
 



If 
$d_{r}(x)=0$
, propagate. Assume that 
$d_{r+1}(x)$
 has four possible values 
$z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4}$
.



 –
 



Assume 
$d_{r+1}(x)=z_{1}$
. Propagate. Get a contradiction.

 
 –
 



Assume 
$d_{r+1}(x)=z_{2}$
. Propagate. Get a contradiction.

 
 –
 



Assume 
$d_{r+1}(x)=z_{3}$
. Propagate. Get a contradiction.

 
 –
 



Assume 
$d_{r+1}(x)=z_{4}$
. Propagate. Get a contradiction.

 


 
 •
 



If 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{1}$
, propagate. Get a contradiction.

 
 •
 



If 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{2}$
, propagate. Get a contradiction.

 



Then we can conclude that 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{3}$
.

The reader can imagine that there could be proofs with even more complex structure. Consider the following.






Proof Structure 4.3
.




Assume that the value of an Adams differential 
$d_{r}(x)$
 has four candidates 
$0,y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}$
.



 •
 



If 
$d_{r}(x)=0$
, propagate. Assume that 
$d_{r+1}(x)$
 has two possible values 
$z,0$
.



 –
 



Assume 
$d_{r+1}(x)=z$
. Propagate. Get a contradiction.

 



We get 
$d_{r+1}(x)=0$
. Assume that 
$d_{r+2}(x)$
 has two possible values 
$w_{1},w_{2}$
.



 –
 



Assume 
$d_{r+2}(x)=w_{1}$
. Propagate. Get a contradiction.

 
 –
 



Assume 
$d_{r+2}(x)=w_{2}$
. Propagate. Get a contradiction.

 


 
 •
 



If 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{1}$
, propagate. Get a contradiction.

 
 •
 



If 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{2}$
, propagate. Get a contradiction.

 



Then we can conclude that 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{3}$
.








A proof with the above structure would be 8 rows from the Table of Proofs with (depth, reason)=(1,T), (2,T), (1,D), (2,T), (2,T), (1,T), (1,T), (0,D). If at some point in the proof we got a permanent cycle, we can even make assumptions about the value of extensions. The reader can find such examples in the Table of Proofs.






Next we describe other types of rows that can be computed or derived from former differentials labeled with different 
reason
s.



 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`d2'
. This means that the 
$d_{2}$
 differential is calculated by 
./Adams
 using secondary Steenrod operation algorithm.

 
 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`N'
. This means that we get a differential from a previous one by naturality.

 
 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`G'
. This means that we get a trivial differential for degree reason.

 
 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`XX'
. This corresponds to the case that if we have 
$d_{r-1}(x)=0$
 then we automatically get 
$d_{r}(x^{2})=0$
.

 
 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`XY'
. This corresponds to the case that if we have 
$d_{r-1}(x)=0$
 then no matter what 
$d_{r}(x)$
 is (when there are two candidates or more), we always get the same value for 
$d_{r}(xy)$
 by Leibniz rule or 
$d_{r}(f(x))$
 by naturality.

 
 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`ToCs'
. This means that in the extension spectral sequence, we have tried all possible values of an extension (linear combinations of elements in the 
$E_{2}$
-page) and the outcome is that certain element in the Adams spectral sequence should survive to the 
$E_{\infty}$
-page and it becomes a summand of the value of the extension.

 
 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`OutCsI'
. This means that in the extensions spectral sequence, two permanent cycles should be the same on the 
$E_{\infty}$
-page. Hence the difference 
dx
 should be hit by an Adams differential. In those rows the columns 
stem,s,t
 refer to the degree of 
dx
 in stead of 
x
.

 
 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`CsCm'
. These are extensions obtained from some simple homotopy relations. For example, if 
$x\in E_{\infty}(S^{0})$
 is the target of an 
$\eta$
-extension, then it should detect a 
$\nu$
-torsion (
$d_{n}^{\nu}(\eta)=0$
 for all 
$n$
) because we know that 
$\eta\cdot\nu=0\in\pi_{*}(S^{0})$
. The reader can find more property of extensions in 
[ 7 ]
.

 
 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`Syn'
. This means that the differential is obtained by the Generalized Leibniz Rule from the former row. The map used in the Generalized Leibniz Rule and the conditions that the machine checked is written in the 
info
 column.

 
 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`SynCs'
. This means that the row is an extension and is obtained by the Generalized Mahowald Trick. The cofiber sequence used in the Generalized Mahowald Trick and the conditions that the machine checked is written in the 
info
 column.

 
 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`SynIn'
. This means that by the Generalized Mahowald Trick we determine that an element in the Adams spectral sequence should be a permanent cycle and become a summand of an extension.

 
 •
 



Rows with 
reason=`TI', `DI', `GI'
. These are essentially the same as rows with 
reason=`T', `D', `G'
 except that the columns 
stem,s,t
 refer to the degree of 
dx
 in stead of 
x
. For consecutive rows with 
reason=`TI'
 the machine are enumerating possible sources of some differential (when we know 
dx
 is hit by some 
$d_{r}$
 differential beforehand) instead of the possible targets and the conclusion row is one with 
reason=`DI'
.

 







Remark 4.4
.




If the reader wants to search for a proof of certain differential, then usually you need to search for the bidegree of the source of the differential with 
reason=‘D’ or reason=‘T’
. Occasionally the machine knows the target should be hit by a certain differential first and deduces the differential by enumerating the possible sources. In this case you also need to search for the bidegree of the target of the differential with 
reason=‘DI’ or reason=‘TI’
.
