

6. 
The Generalized Leibniz Rule and Generalized Mahowald Trick





Now, we introduce the theorem of the Generalized Leibniz Rule, a valuable tool for computing new Adams differentials.






Theorem 6.1
 
(Generalized Leibniz Rule)
.




Let $f:X\to Y$ be a map between two classical spectra.
Suppose that $2\leq n\leq r$ , $e(f)\leq m\leq n-2+e(f)$ , $l\geq e(f)$ , and we have










$x\in Z_{r-1}^{s,t}(X),\hskip 14.22636pty\in Z_{r-1-m+e(f)}^{s+m,t+m}(Y)$















$x_{\infty}\in Z_{\infty}^{s+r,t+r-1}(X),\hskip 14.22636pty_{\infty}\in Z_{\infty}^{s+r+l,t+r+l-1}(Y)$







and the following conditions hold:



 (1)
 



$d_{r}(x)=x_{\infty}$
,

 
 (2)
 



$d_{m}^{f,E_{n}}(x)=y$
,

 
 (3)
 



$d_{l}^{f,E_{\infty}}(x_{\infty})=y_{\infty}$
,

 
 (4)
 



the differential in (1) has no crossing on the 
$E_{n}$
-page or (2) has no crossing.

 
 (5)
 



the differential in (3) has no crossing.

 



Then we have an Adams differential










$d_{r+l-m}(y)=y_{\infty}.$













Proof.




Consider the commutative diagram of synthetic spectra










$\textstyle{\Sigma^{0,e(f)}\nu X/\lambda^{n-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$ $\scriptstyle{\hat{f}_{n-1}}$ $\scriptstyle{\delta_{X}}$ $\textstyle{\nu Y/\lambda^{n-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$ $\scriptstyle{\delta_{Y}}$ $\textstyle{\Sigma^{1,-n+1+e(f)}\nu X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$ $\scriptstyle{\hat{f}}$ $\textstyle{\Sigma^{1,-n+1}\nu Y.}$







By condition (1) and Proposition 4.6 , we have







(6.2)





$d^{\delta_{X}}_{r}(x)=\lambda^{r-n}x_{\infty}.$







By conditions (2) and (3), and Definition 5.4 , we have







(6.3)





$d^{\hat{f}_{n-1}}_{m}(x)=\lambda^{m-e(f)}y$







and










$d^{\hat{f}}_{l}(x_{\infty})=\lambda^{l-e(f)}y_{\infty},$







which implies







(6.4)





$d^{\hat{f}}_{l}(\lambda^{r-n}x_{\infty})=\lambda^{r+l-n-e(f)}y_{\infty}.$











Applying Proposition 4.16 and Proposition 5.10 to conditions (4) and (5), we know that the differential in ( 6.2 ) or ( 6.3 ) has no crossing, and that the differential in ( 6.4 ) has no crossing.






Therefore, we can apply Corollary 2.15 and obtain










$d^{\delta_{Y}}_{r+l-m}(\lambda^{m-e(f)}y)=\lambda^{r+l-n-e(f)}y_{\infty}.$







By Remark 4.12 , this is equivalent to










$d_{r+l-m}(y)=y_{\infty}.$







∎








Remark 6.5
.




We can further generalize Theorem 
6.1
 by using the conditions in Theorem 
2.12
 rather than those Corollary 
2.15
. We leave this generalization to the reader.








We emphasize that the no-crossing conditions in Theorem 
6.1
, the Generalized Leibniz Rule, are crucial. We first demonstrate the strength of the Generalized Leibniz Rule with Example 
6.7
, which uses a 
$d_{2}$
-differential to prove a 
$d_{3}$
-differential in the classical Adams spectral sequence. Additionally, Example 
6.8
 shows that, without the no-crossing condition, the conclusion could be false.






Remark 6.6
.




A version of the Generalized Leibniz Rule without the no-crossing conditions is presented in the synthetic setting in Chua’s work 
[ 19 , Theorem 12.9]
. However, there is no doubt that this version is incorrect. Indeed, Example 
6.8
 is a counter-example to Chua’s theorem. For further details, see Remark 
6.10
.

Example 6.8
.




We show that, without the no crossing conditions, the Generalized Leibniz Rule could lead to incorrect conclusions.






Consider the map 
$f=2:S^{0}\rightarrow S^{0}$
, and the classical Hopf invariant one differential










$d_{2}(h_{4})=h_{0}h_{3}^{2}.$







Set










$n=2,\ r=2,\ s=1,\ t=16,\ m=1,\ l=2,\ e(f)=1,$







and










$x=h_{4}\in\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{1,16}\cong Z_{1}^{1,16}(S^{0}),\ y=h_{0}h_{4}\in\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{2,17}\cong Z_{1}^{2,17}(S^{0}),$















$x_{\infty}=h_{0}h_{3}^{2}\in\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{3,17}\cong Z_{\infty}^{3,17}(S^{0}),\ y_{\infty}=0\in\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{5,19}\cong Z_{\infty}^{5,19}(S^{0}).$







In terms of the conditions in Theorem 
6.1
, we have:



 (1)
 



$d_{2}(h_{4})=h_{0}h_{3}^{2}$
,

 
 (2)
 



$d_{1}^{f,E_{2}}(h_{4})=h_{0}h_{4}$
, since the product of 
$h_{4}$

$h_{0}$
 is 
$h_{0}h_{4}$
 in Ext,

 
 (3)
 



$d_{2}^{f,E_{\infty}}(h_{0}h_{3}^{2})=0$
, from Example 
5.8
(4),

 
 (4)
 




 (a)
 



the differential in (1) has no crossing, from Remark 
4.17
, and

 
 (b)
 



the differential in (2) has no crossing, for degree reasons,

 


 
 (5)
 



the differential in (3) has a crossing:










$d_{1}^{f,E_{\infty}}(d_{0})=h_{0}d_{0},$







from Example 
5.13
(4).

$(5)$
, if there were no crossing for the differential in 
$(3)$
, then the Generalized Leibniz Rule would imply a classical Adams differential










$d_{3}(h_{0}h_{4})=0,$







which is incorrect. This example illustrates that the no-crossing conditions are essential when applying the Generalized Leibniz Rule.








Remark 6.9
.




As noted in Example 
5.8
(4) and Example 
5.13
(4), the essential 
$(f,E_{\infty})$
-extension:










$d_{1}^{f,E_{\infty}}(d_{0})=h_{0}d_{0},$







is a crossing for both the 
$(f,E_{\infty})$
-extension,










$d_{2}^{f,E_{\infty}}(h_{0}h_{3}^{2})=0,$







and the inessential 
$(f,E_{\infty})$
-extension:










$d_{2}^{f,E_{\infty}}(h_{0}h_{3}^{2})=h_{0}d_{0}.$







This indicates that, in Example 
6.8
, if we were to disregard the no-crossing condition (5), and apply the Generalized Leibniz Rule to these two cases of the 
$(f,E_{\infty})$
-extensions, we would arrive at two conflicting classical statements:










$d_{3}(h_{0}h_{4})=0,\ d_{3}(h_{0}h_{4})=h_{0}d_{0}.$













Remark 6.10
.




In Chua’s work 
[ 19 , Theorem 12.9]
, it is stated that for a synthetic map 
$\alpha:X\rightarrow Y$
, and an element 
$x\in\pi_{*,*}X/\lambda$
, there exists a differential from a maximal 
$\alpha$
-extension of 
$x$
 to a maximal 
$\alpha$
-extension of 
$d_{r}(x)$
. According to 
[ 19 , Definition 12.5]
, a maximal 
$\alpha$
-extension of 
$x^{\prime}$
 is defined as 
$\alpha[x^{\prime}]$
, where 
$[x^{\prime}]$
 represents a lift of 
$x^{\prime}$
 to the 
$E_{r^{\prime}}$
-page, chosen such that 
$\alpha[x^{\prime}]$
 is the most 
$\lambda$
-divisible among all such lifts.






In the context of Example 
6.8
, let 
$r=2,r^{\prime}=\infty$
,










$\alpha=[h_{0}]:S^{0,1}\rightarrow S^{0,0},$







and consider 
$x=h_{4}$
 in Ext, with 
$x^{\prime}=d_{2}(x)=h_{0}h_{3}^{2}$
.






As noted in Example 
5.8
(4), the 
$(f,E_{\infty})$
-extension:










$d_{2}^{f,E_{\infty}}(h_{0}h_{3}^{2})=0$







is equivalent to the existence of a synthetic homotopy class 
$[h_{0}h_{3}^{2}]$
 in 
$\pi_{14,17}S^{0,0}$
, such that










$[h_{0}h_{3}^{2}]\cdot[h_{0}]=0\ \text{in}\ \pi_{14,18}S^{0,0}.$







Similarly, the inessential 
$(f,E_{\infty})$
-extension:










$d_{2}^{f,E_{\infty}}(h_{0}h_{3}^{2})=h_{0}d_{0}$







implies the existence of another synthetic homotopy class 
$[h_{0}h_{3}^{2}]$
 in 
$\pi_{14,17}S^{0,0}$
, such that










$[h_{0}h_{3}^{2}]\cdot[h_{0}]=[h_{0}d_{0}]\ \text{in}\ \pi_{14,18}S^{0,0}.$







Between these two lifts of 
$h_{0}h_{3}^{2}$
, the first 
$[h_{0}h_{3}^{2}]$
 is clearly the maximal 
$[h_{0}]$
-extension according to Chua’s definition 
[ 19 , Definition 12.5]
. Consequently, the incorrect version of the Generalized Leibniz Rule in 
[ 19 , Theorem 12.9]
, would lead to an incorrect conclusion:










$d_{3}(h_{0}h_{4})=0.$













Next, we discuss the Generalized Mahowald Trick.






In order to apply the Generalized Leibniz Rule, we need to provide a method for computing extensions on specific Adams 
$E_{k}$
-pages, such as the 
$(f,E_{3})$
-extension 
$d_{2}^{f,E_{3}}(h_{0}h_{4}^{2})=h_{0}p$
 in Example 
6.7
. This is provided by Theorem 
6.12
 (the Generalized Mahowald Trick). The crux of the proof of the Generalized Mahowald Trick lies in the following lemma.






Lemma 6.11
 
(May 
[ 46 ]
)
.




Let $X\to Y\to Z\to\Sigma X$ and $X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}\to\Sigma X^{\prime}$ be distinguished triangles of (synthetic) spectra. By smashing these distinguished triangles together, we obtain the following commutative diagram of cofiber sequences:

If $a\in\pi_{n}(X\wedge Z^{\prime})$ and $b\in\pi_{n}(Y\wedge Y^{\prime})$ map to the same element in $\pi_{n}(Y\wedge Z^{\prime})$ , then there exists $c\in\pi_{n}(Z\wedge X^{\prime})$ such that



 (1)
 



$b$

$c$

$\pi_{n}(Z\wedge Y^{\prime})$
, and

 
 (2)
 



$a$
 and 
$c$

$\pi_{n-1}(X\wedge X^{\prime})$
.

 









Proof.




The proof follows directly from [ 46 , Lemma 4.6] .






In fact, consider $V$ in Axiom TC3 of [ 46 , Section 4] and the corresponding commutative diagram. By [ 46 , Lemma 4.6] we know that $V$ is the pull back of the following diagram.










$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$ $\scriptstyle{\lrcorner}$ $\textstyle{Y\wedge Y^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$ $\textstyle{X\wedge Z^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$ $\textstyle{Y\wedge Z^{\prime}}$

Then we let $c=j_{3}(v)\in\pi_{n}(Z\wedge X^{\prime})$ , where $j_{3}:V\rightarrow Z\wedge X^{\prime}$ is the map in the commutative diagram in Axiom TC3 of [ 46 , Section 4] . This lemma follows from the commutativity of the diagram.
∎

We refer to Theorem 
6.12
 as the Generalized Mahowald Trick, as this approach was first utilized by Mahowald and his collaborators in various works (see, for example, 
[ 44 ]
), particularly in the case where 
$m_{1}=l_{1}=0$
. The synthetic setting advances this method by allowing for the consideration of cases where 
$m_{1}>0,l_{1}>0$
 as well.






Remark 6.17
.




Several versions of classical generalizations of Mahowald’s original trick appear in the literature and are often referred to as geometric boundary theorems. Notable examples include the works of Behrens 
[ 8 ]
, and Ma 
[ 41 ]
.








Remark 6.18
.




A synthetic generalization of the Mahowald Trick, again lacking any no-crossing conditions, is also presented in Chua’s work 
[ 19 , Theorem 12.11]
. This version is also incorrect for similar reasons.

Proposition 6.20
.




Suppose that we have an $(f,E_{r})$ -extension $d^{f,E_{r}}_{n}(x)=y$ , where $x\in Z_{r-1}^{s,t}(X)$ and $y\in Z_{r-1-n+e(f)}^{s+n,t+n}(Y)$ . Then for all $2\leq r^{\prime}<r$ , we also have







(6.21)





$d^{f,E_{r^{\prime}}}_{n}(x)=y.$











Furthermore, if $d^{f,E_{r}}_{n}(x)=y$ is essential and $n\leq r^{\prime}-2+e(f)$ , then ( 6.21 ) is inessential if and only if there exists some $0<a^{\prime}\leq n-e(f)$ and an element










$x^{\prime}\in Z_{r^{\prime}-1-a^{\prime}}^{s+a^{\prime},t+a^{\prime}}(X)\backslash Z_{r-1-a^{\prime}}^{s+a^{\prime},t+a^{\prime}}(X)$







such that










$d^{\hat{f}_{r^{\prime}-1}}_{n-a^{\prime}-b}(\lambda^{a^{\prime}}x^{\prime})=\lambda^{n-b-e(f)}y^{\prime}$







for some $b\geq 0$ .








Proof.




Consider the following commutative diagram:

By Corollary 2.18 , $(\rho_{X},\rho_{Y})$ induces a map from the $\hat{f}_{r-1}$ -ESS to the $\hat{f}_{r^{\prime}-1}$ -ESS. Therefore, by naturality,










$d^{\hat{f}_{r-1}}_{n}(x)=\lambda^{n-e(f)}y\ \text{implies}\ d^{\hat{f}_{r^{\prime}-1}}_{n}(x)=\lambda^{n-e(f)}y,$







which, by definition, is










$d^{f,E_{r^{\prime}}}_{n}(x)=y.$











Next, we prove the second part of the proposition. By Definition 5.4 , the $(f,E_{r})$ -extension ( 6.21 ) is inessential if and only if
there exists $0<a\leq n-e(f)$ and










$\lambda^{a}x^{\prime}\in E_{\infty}^{s+a,t+a,t}(\nu X/\lambda^{r^{\prime}-1})\cong Z_{r^{\prime}-1-a}^{s+a,t+a}(X)/B_{1+a}^{s+a,t+a}(X),$

$d^{\hat{f}_{r^{\prime}-1}}_{n-a}(\lambda^{a}x^{\prime})=\lambda^{n-e(f)}y,$

The following Corollary 
6.23

Corollary 6.23
.




Suppose $r\geq r^{\prime}$ and there exists an $(f,E_{r^{\prime}})$ -extension










$d^{f,E_{r^{\prime}}}_{n}(x)=y$







for $x\in Z_{r-1}^{s,t}(X)$ and $y\in Z_{r^{\prime}-1-n+e(f)}^{s+n,t+n}(Y)$ . Assume that this extension has no crossing of the form $d^{f,E_{r^{\prime}-a}}_{n-a-b}(x^{\prime})=y^{\prime}$ for any $b>0$ , $0<a\leq n-e(f)$ , and










$x^{\prime}\in Z_{r^{\prime}-1-a}^{s+a,t+a}(X)\backslash Z_{r-1-a}^{s+a,t+a}(X).$







Under these conditions, we also have an $(f,E_{r})$ -extension










$d^{f,E_{r}}_{n}(x)=y.$
