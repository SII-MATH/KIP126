

2. 
A Spectral Sequence for Extensions





In this paper, we assume that all symbols 
$X,Y,Z,W,\dots$
 for spectra represent 2-completed connective spectra of finite type. In particular, their 
$\mathrm{H}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}$
-Adams spectral sequences converge strongly.






Consider a map 
$f:X\to Y$
 between two spectra and we can construct the following chain complex










$0\to\pi_{*}X\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}f}\pi_{*}Y\to 0$







whose homology is










$\Ker(\pi_{*}f)\oplus\Coker(\pi_{*}f).$







We encounter 
$f$
-extension problems when analyzing the homotopy groups through the 
$E_{\infty}$
-pages of the 
$\mathrm{H}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}$
-based Adams spectral sequences. To address these extensions, we introduce the following spectral sequence.






Definition 2.1
.




For a map 
$f:X\to Y$
, we define an 
$f$
-extension spectral sequence (denoted 
$f$
-ESS) as the spectral sequence derived from filtering the above chain complex according to the Adams filtration on 
$\pi_{*}$
. The 
$E_{0}$
-page of this spectral sequence is isomorphic to the direct sum of 
$E_{\infty}$
-pages of the Adams spectral sequences of 
$X$
 and 
$Y$
:










$\prescript{f\!}{}{E}_{0}^{s,t}\cong E_{\infty}^{s,t}(X)\oplus E_{\infty}^{s,t}(Y)\Longrightarrow\Ker(\pi_{*}f)\oplus\Coker(\pi_{*}f)$







where the 
$d_{0}$
-differential is induced by 
$f$
 on the 
$E_{\infty}$
-pages.






Differentials in this spectral sequence has the form










$d_{n}^{f}:\prescript{f\!}{}{E}_{n}^{s,t}\to\prescript{f\!}{}{E}_{n}^{s+n,t+n}.$













To prevent confusion between differentials 
$d_{n}$
 in the Adams spectral sequence of a spectrum 
$X$
 and those in the 
$f$
-extension spectral sequence, we denote the latter with a superscript, writing them as 
$d_{n}^{f}$
.






In the remainder of the paper, we denote by 
$\mathrm{AF}(f)$
 the Adams filtration of a map 
$f$
. Recall that 
$\mathrm{AF}(f)\geq k$
 if and only if there exist 
$k$
 composable maps 
$f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}$
, each inducing a trivial map in 
$\mathrm{H}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}$
-homology, such that 
$f$
 is homotopic to the composition 
$f_{k}\circ\cdots\circ f_{1}$
 (see 
[ 51 , Theorem 2.2.14]
 for example).






Notation 2.2
.




Let 
$\prescript{f\!}{}{Z}^{s,t}_{n}(X)\subset E^{s,t}_{\infty}(X)$
 be the subgroup consisting of elements for which the 
$f$
-extension differentials 
$d^{f}_{0},\dots,d^{f}_{n}$
 vanish.
Let 
$\prescript{f\!}{}{B}^{s,t}_{n}(Y)\subset E^{s,t}_{\infty}(Y)$
 be the subgroup generated by the sum of images of the 
$f$
-extension differentials 
$d^{f}_{0},\dots,d^{f}_{n}$
. We define 
$\prescript{f\!}{}{B}^{s,t}_{-1}(Y)=0$
 and 
$\prescript{f\!}{}{Z}^{s,t}_{-1}(X)=E^{s,t}_{\infty}(X)$
.








By definition we have










$\prescript{f\!}{}{E}^{s,t}_{n}\cong\prescript{f\!}{}{Z}^{s,t}_{n-1}(X)\oplus\big(E^{s,t}_{\infty}(Y)/\prescript{f\!}{}{B}^{s,t}_{n-1}(Y)\big).$











A nonzero 
$d^{f}_{n}$
 differential takes the form










$d^{f}_{n}:\prescript{f\!}{}{Z}^{s,t}_{n-1}(X)\to E^{s+n,t+n}_{\infty}(Y)/\prescript{f\!}{}{B}^{s+n,t+n}_{n-1}(Y).$







When we write










$d^{f}_{n}(x)=y$







for 
$x\in E_{\infty}^{s,t}(X)$
 and 
$y\in E_{\infty}^{s+n,t+n}(Y)$
, it is understood that 
$x$
 belongs to 
$\prescript{f\!}{}{Z}^{s,t}_{n-1}(X)$
, and










$d^{f}_{n}(x)=y+\prescript{f\!}{}{B}^{s+n,t+n}_{n-1}(Y).$







Furthermore, we may sometimes write










$d^{f}_{n}(x)\equiv y\mod M$







for some subgroup 
$M\subset E_{\infty}^{s+n,t+n}(Y)$
. This means that










$d^{f}_{n}(x)=y+m+\prescript{f\!}{}{B}^{s+n,t+n}_{n-1}(Y)$







for some 
$m\in M$
.








Definition 2.3
.




We say there is an 
$f$
-extension from 
$x\in E_{\infty}^{s,t}(X)$
 to 
$y\in E_{\infty}^{s+n,t+n}(Y)$
 if 
$d_{n}^{f}(x)=y$
 in the 
$f$
-ESS. We say this 
$f$
-extension is 
essential
 if 
$y$
 is nontrivial in the 
$E_{n}$
-page of the 
$f$
-ESS, or equivalently










$y\notin\prescript{f\!}{}{B}^{s+n,t+n}_{n-1}(Y).$







Otherwise we refer to it as inessential.








Notation 2.4
.




For 
$x\in E_{\infty}^{s,t}(X)$
, we denote by 
$\{x\}$
 the set of all classes in 
$\pi_{t-s}X$
 that are detected by 
$x$
. We use 
$[x]$
 to refer to a specific class or a general class in 
$\{x\}$
, with the choice understood from the context.








Proposition 2.5
.




Consider $f:X\to Y$ , $x\in E_{\infty}^{s,t}(X)$ , $y\in E_{\infty}^{s+n,t+n}(Y)$ and $y^{\prime}\in E_{\infty}^{s+m,t+m}(Y)$ for $m,n\geq 0$ .



 (1)
 



There is an 
$f$
-extension from 
$x$
 to 
$y$
, i.e., 
$d_{n}^{f}(x)=y$
 in the 
$f$
-ESS if and only if there is a class 
$[x]\in\{x\}$
 such that 
$f[x]\in\{y\}$
.

 
 (2)
 



An 
$f$
-extension from 
$x$
 to 
$y$
 is inessential, i.e., 
$y$
 is trivial in the 
$E_{n}$
-page of the 
$f$
-ESS if and only if there exists an element 
$x^{\prime}\in E_{\infty}^{s+a,t+a}(X)$
 for 
$0<a\leq n$
 such that we have an essential differential 
$d_{n-a}^{f}(x^{\prime})=y$
. Equivalently, there exists a class 
$[x^{\prime}]\in\{x^{\prime}\}\subset\pi_{*}X$
 with AF
$(x^{\prime})>$
AF
$(x)$
, such that 
$f[x^{\prime}]\in\{y\}$
.

 
 (3)
 



Suppose we have an 
$f$
-extension from 
$x$
 to both 
$y$
 and 
$y^{\prime}$
.



 (a)
 



If 
$m=n$
, then 
$y-y^{\prime}\in\prescript{f\!}{}{B}^{s+n,t+n}_{n-1}(Y)$
 and there exists an element 
$x^{\prime}\in E_{\infty}^{s+a,t+a}(X)$
 for 
$0<a\leq n$
 such that we have an essential differential










$d_{n-a}^{f}(x^{\prime})=y-y^{\prime}$







in 
$f$
-ESS. Equivalently, there exists a class 
$[x^{\prime}]\in\{x^{\prime}\}\subset\pi_{*}X$
 with AF
$(x^{\prime})>$
AF
$(x)$
, such that 
$f[x^{\prime}]\in\{y-y^{\prime}\}$
.

 
 (b)
 



If 
$m>n$
, then the 
$f$
-extension from 
$x$
 to 
$y$
 is inessential.

 


 









Proof.




Part (1) is straightforward from the setup of the extension spectral sequence and Definition  2.3 . Parts (2) and (3) follow from Part (1).
∎








Corollary 2.6
.




If the Adams filtration of $f$ is $k$ , then
 $d^{f}_{i}=0$ for $i<k$ .








Proof.




When $k>0$ , it is clear that $d^{f}_{0}=0$ , which means that for any $s$ and $[x]\in F_{s}\pi_{*}(X)$ , we have $f([x])\in F_{s+1}\pi_{*}(Y)$ . Since $\mathrm{AF}(f)=k$ , we know that there exist $k$ maps










$X=X_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{k}}X_{k}=Y$







such that each $f_{i}$ induces trivial map in $\mathrm{H}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}$ -homology (hence $\mathrm{AF}(f_{i})>0$ ) and $f$ is homotopic to the composition $f_{k}\circ\cdots\circ f_{1}.$ 
Then for any $s$ and $[x]\in F_{s}\pi_{*}(X)$ , inductively we have










$f_{1}([x])\in F_{s+1}\pi_{*}(X_{1})$















$f_{2}\circ f_{1}([x])\in F_{s+2}\pi_{*}(X_{2})$















$\cdots$















$f([x])=f_{k}\circ\cdots\circ f_{1}([x])\in F_{s+k}\pi_{*}(X_{k})=F_{s+k}\pi_{*}(Y).$







By Proposition 2.5 .(1) this means that $d_{i}^{f}x=0$ for any $x\in E_{\infty}^{*,*}(X)$ and $i<k$ .
∎








Definition 2.7
.





 (1)
 



For 
$x\in E_{\infty}^{s,t}(X)$
, we say that the an 
$f$
-extension 
$d_{n}^{f}(x)=y$
 has a crossing that hits Adams filtration 
$p$
 for some 
$p\leq s+n$
, if there exists an element 
$x^{\prime}\in E_{\infty}^{s+a,t+a}(X)$
 with 
$a>0$
 and an 
$f$
-extension 
$d_{m}^{f}(x^{\prime})=y^{\prime}$
 for 
$0\neq y^{\prime}\in E^{s+a+m,t+a+m}_{\infty}(Y)$
 such that










$p=\textup{AF}(y^{\prime})=s+a+m\leq\textup{AF}(y)=s+n.$






 
 (2)
 



We say that an 
$f$
-extension 
$d_{n}^{f}(x)=y$
 has no crossing that hits the range AF 
$\geq p$

$x^{\prime}\in E_{\infty}^{s+a,t+a}(X)$
 with 
$a>0$
 and an 
$f$
-extension 
$d_{m}^{f}(x^{\prime})=y^{\prime}$
 for 
$0\neq y^{\prime}\in E^{s+a+m,t+a+m}_{\infty}(Y)$
 such that










$p\leq\textup{AF}(y^{\prime})=s+a+m\leq\textup{AF}(y)=s+n.$






 
 (3)
 



We say that an 
$f$
-extension 
$d_{n}^{f}(x)=y$
 has no crossing if it has no crossing that hits the range AF
$\geq\mathrm{AF}(x)+1$
.

Remark 2.8
.




The following statements are equivalent.



 •
 



An 
$f$
-extension 
$d_{n}^{f}(x)=y$
 has no crossing that hits the range AF
$\geq p$
.

 
 •
 



If for any 
$a>0$
 there is an 
$f$
-extension from 
$x^{\prime}\in E_{\infty}^{s+a,t+a}(X)$
 to some nontrivial 
$y^{\prime}$
, then AF
$(y^{\prime})<p$
 or AF
$(y^{\prime})>$
AF
$(y)$
.

 









Remark 2.9
.




We do not require the crossings in Definition 
2.7
 (1)(2) to be essential. A crossing implies the existence of an essential crossing, possibly a shorter one.








Proposition 2.10
.




An $f$ -extension from $x$ to $y$ has no crossing in the range $\mathrm{AF}\geq p$ if and only if for all $[x]\in\{x\}$ such that $\mathrm{AF}(f[x])\geq p$ we have $f[x]\in\{y\}$ . In particular, we have



 (1)
 



An 
$f$
-extension from 
$x$
 to 
$y$
 has no crossing if and only if for all 
$[x]\in\{x\}$
 we have 
$f[x]\in\{y\}$
.

 
 (2)
 



An 
$f$
-extension 
$d_{n}^{f}(x)=0$
 has no crossing if and only if for all 
$[x]\in\{x\}$
,










$\mathrm{AF}(f[x])>\mathrm{AF}(x)+n.$






 
 (3)
 



If 
$\mathrm{AF}(f)=n$
, then all 
$d^{f}_{n}$
-differentials have no crossing in the 
$f$
-ESS.

 









Proof.




First we prove the only if part. Assume by contradiction that there exists $[x]\in\{x\}$ such that $f[x]=[y^{\prime}]\notin\{y\}$ is detected by $y^{\prime}\in E_{\infty}(Y)$ with $\mathrm{AF}(y^{\prime})\geq p$ . Then there is an $f$ extension from $x$ to $y^{\prime}$ . By Proposition 2.5  (3), $y^{\prime}$ (if $\mathrm{AF}(y)>\mathrm{AF}(y^{\prime})$ ) or $y-y^{\prime}$ (if $\mathrm{AF}(y)=\mathrm{AF}(y^{\prime})$ ) or $y$ (if $\mathrm{AF}(y)<\mathrm{AF}(y^{\prime})$ ) should be hit by a shorter $d^{f}$ differential. This shorter differential is a crossing of the $f$ -extension from $x$ to $y$ that hits $\mathrm{AF}\geq p$ , which is a contradiction. Now we prove the if part. Assume that the $f$ -extension has a crossing from $x^{\prime}$ to $y^{\prime}$ with $p\leq\mathrm{AF}(y^{\prime})\leq\mathrm{AF}(y)$ . Then there exists $[x^{\prime}]\in\{x^{\prime}\}$ such that $f([x^{\prime}])\in\{y^{\prime}\}$ . Note that $[x]+[x^{\prime}]\in\{x\}$ since $\mathrm{AF}(x^{\prime})>\mathrm{AF}(x)$ , we have










$f([x]+[x^{\prime}])=f([x])+f([x^{\prime}])=[y]+[y^{\prime}]\in\begin{cases}\{y^{\prime}\}&\text{ if }\mathrm{AF}(y^{\prime})<\mathrm{AF}(y)\\
\{y+y^{\prime}\}&\text{ if }\mathrm{AF}(y^{\prime})=\mathrm{AF}(y).\\
\end{cases}$







However, by assumption we should have $f([x]+[x^{\prime}])\in\{y\}$ and it is contradictory to the equation above in either case.






Hence the main statement is true. Part (1) (2) are special cases of the main statement and Part (3) holds for degree reasons.
∎

(2.13)





$p[x]\in\{z\}$







and







(2.14)





$f[x]\in\{y\}.$

Assume $\prescript{p\!}{}{E}_{0}^{*,*}=\prescript{p\!}{}{E}_{r}^{*,*}$ and $\prescript{q\!}{}{E}_{0}^{*,*}=\prescript{q\!}{}{E}_{r}^{*,*}$ for some $r\geq 0$ . Then










$(d^{p}_{r},d^{q}_{r}):E_{\infty}^{*,*}(X)\oplus E_{\infty}^{*,*}(Y)\to E_{\infty}^{*,*}(Z)\oplus E_{\infty}^{*,*}(W)$







induces a map from the $f$ -ESS to the $g$ -ESS.








Proof.




Since $\prescript{p\!}{}{E}_{0}^{*,*}=\prescript{p\!}{}{E}_{r}^{*,*}$ and $\prescript{q\!}{}{E}_{0}^{*,*}=\prescript{q\!}{}{E}_{r}^{*,*}$ , we know that $d^{p}_{r}$ , $d^{q}_{r}$ have no crossings. By Corollary 2.15 , we have $d^{g}_{0}\circ d^{p}_{r}=d^{q}_{r}\circ d^{f}_{0}$ and therefore $(d^{p}_{r},d^{q}_{r})$ induces a map from $\prescript{f\!}{}{E}_{1}^{*,*}$ to $\prescript{g\!}{}{E}_{1}^{*,*}$ . Inductively we can apply Corollary 2.15 again and show that $(d^{p}_{r},d^{q}_{r})$ induces a map from $\prescript{f\!}{}{E}_{n}^{*,*}$ to $\prescript{g\!}{}{E}_{n}^{*,*}$ and $d^{g}_{n}\circ d^{p}_{r}=d^{q}_{r}\circ d^{f}_{n}$ for all $n$ .
∎








Corollary 2.19
.




If the composition $g\circ f$ of two maps $f:X\to Y$ , $g:Y\to Z$ is trivial, and $d_{n}^{f}(x)=y$ for $x\in E_{\infty}^{s,t}(X)$ and $y\in E_{\infty}^{s+n,t+n}(Y)$ , then $y$ is a permanent cycle in the $g$ -ESS, i.e., we have
 $d^{g}_{m}(y)=0$ for all $m\geq 0$ .








Proof.




This follows immediately from the following commutative diagram










$\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$ $\scriptstyle{f}$ $\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$ $\scriptstyle{g}$ $\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$ $\textstyle{Z}$







and Corollary 2.15 .






There is a second short proof using homotopy groups. By Proposition 2.5 , we know that there exists $[x]\in\{x\}$ such that $f([x])\in\{y\}$ . Let $[y]=f([x])\in\{y\}$ and we have $g([y])=g(f([x]))=0$ . Hence $y$ is a permanent $d^{g}$ cycle.
∎








Proposition 2.20
.




Suppose that the sequence of spectra










$X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z$







induces a sequence of homotopy groups










$\pi_{*}X\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}f}\pi_{*}Y\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}g}\pi_{*}Z$







that is exact in $\pi_{*}Y$ . Then all permanent cycles in $E^{*,*}_{\infty}(Y)$ of the $g$ -ESS are boundaries in the $f$ -ESS.








Proof.




If we consider the following sequence







(2.21)





$0\to\pi_{*}X\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}f}\pi_{*}Y\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}g}\pi_{*}Z\to 0$







and treat it as a chain complex filtered by the Adams filtration, we obtain an extension spectral sequence comprising $d^{f}$ and $d^{g}$ differentials. The abutment of this spectral sequence, when projected onto the $Y$ component, is zero. Therefor, all permanent $d^{g}$ -cycles are killed by $d^{f}$ -differentials.






Again we offer another proof via homotopy groups. Assume that $y\in E^{*,*}_{\infty}(Y)$ is a permanent $d^{g}$ cycle. By the convergence of ESS we know that it detects an $g$ -torsion $[y]\in\{y\}$ with $g([y])=0$ . Hence there exists $[x]\in\pi_{*}X$ such that $f([x])=[y]$ by exactness. Assume that $[x]$ is detected by $x\in E^{*,*}_{\infty}(X)$ . Then there exists an $f$ extension from $x$ to $y$ .
∎
