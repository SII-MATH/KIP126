

3. 
Adams spectral sequences and Extension spectral sequences





In this section we describe how the computer program 
./ss
 organizes the data of the Adams spectral sequence and the extension spectral sequence and how to read the differentials and extensions generated by the computer program.






The program 
./ss
 organizes the Adams spectral sequence by the following.










$0=B_{1}^{s,t}\subseteq B_{2}^{s,t}\subseteq B_{3}^{s,t}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq Z_{3}^{s,t}\subseteq Z_{2}^{s,t}\subseteq E_{2}^{s,t}$







where 
$B_{r}$
 is the subgroup of the image of 
$d_{r}$
 differentials and 
$Z_{r}$
 is the subgroup of 
$d_{r}$
-cycles. We can interpret Adams differentials as isomorphisms







(3.1)





$d_{r}:Z_{r-1}^{s,t}/Z_{r}^{s,t}\xrightarrow{~~\cong~~}B_{r}^{s+r,t+r-1}/B_{r-1}^{s+r,t+r-1}$







and therefore we can also consider the inverse







(3.2)





$d_{r}^{-1}:B_{r}^{s,t}/B_{r-1}^{s,t}\xrightarrow{~~\cong~~}Z_{r-1}^{s-r,t-r+1}/Z_{r}^{s-r,t-r+1}.$

We write differentials in terms of elements in 
$E_{2}^{*,*}$
. The value of 
$d_{r}^{\pm 1}$
 should be viewed as a coset. By (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) we see that



 •
 



if we get 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{1}$
 and 
$d_{r}(x)=y_{2}$
 for two different reasons, then we have 
$y_{1}-y_{2}\in B_{r-1}$
;

 
 •
 



if we get 
$d_{r}^{-1}(x)=z_{1}$
 and 
$d_{r}^{-1}(x)=z_{2}$
 for two different reasons, then we have 
$z_{1}-z_{2}\in Z_{r}$
.

 







Consider a cofiber sequence










$X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z.$







The program 
./ss
 organizes the extension spectral sequence (see 
[ 7 ]
) by the following.










${B}_{\infty}^{s,t}\subseteq\prescript{f\!}{}{B}_{0}^{s,t}\subseteq\prescript{f\!}{}{B}_{1}^{s,t}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\prescript{g\!}{}{Z}_{1}^{s,t}\subseteq\prescript{g\!}{}{Z}_{0}^{s,t}\subseteq{Z}_{\infty}^{s,t}.$







where 
$B_{r}$
 is the subgroup of the image of 
$d_{r}$
 differentials and 
$Z_{r}$
 is the subgroup of 
$d_{r}$
-cycles. We can interpret extensions as isomorphisms







(3.3)





$d_{r}^{f}:\prescript{f\!}{}{Z}_{r-1}^{s,t}(X)/\prescript{f\!}{}{Z}_{r}^{s,t}(X)\xrightarrow{~~\cong~~}\prescript{f\!}{}{B}_{r}^{s+r,t+r}(Y)/\prescript{f\!}{}{B}_{r-1}^{s+r,t+r}(Y)$







when 
$r>0$
 and







(3.4)





$d_{0}^{f}:Z_{\infty}^{s,t}(X)/\prescript{f\!}{}{Z}_{0}^{s,t}(X)\xrightarrow{~~\cong~~}\prescript{f\!}{}{B}_{0}^{s+r,t+r}(Y)/B_{\infty}^{s+r,t+r}(Y)$







when 
$r=0$
.






We write differentials in terms of elements in 
$E_{2}^{*,*}$
. The value of 
$(d_{r}^{f})^{\pm 1}$
 should be viewed as a coset. By (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) we see that



 •
 



if 
$r>0$
 and we get 
$d_{r}^{f}(x)=y_{1}$
 and 
$d_{r}^{f}(x)=y_{2}$
 for two different reasons, then we have 
$y_{1}-y_{2}\in\prescript{f\!}{}{B}_{r-1}(Y)$
;

 
 •
 



if 
$r=0$
 and we get 
$d_{0}^{f}(x)=y_{1}$
 and 
$d_{0}^{f}(x)=y_{2}$
 for two different reasons, then we have 
$y_{1}-y_{2}\in B_{\infty}(Y)$
.

 
 •
 



we leave it to the reader to think about what happens to 
$(d^{f}_{r})^{-1}$
 in similar situations.

 







The program 
./ss
 can compute a lot differentials and extensions based on the data structure we describe above and various theorems such as the Leibniz rule and naturality. The proofs are given in the Table of Proofs (Section 
4
). There are three manually added differentials: 
$d_{5}h_{0}^{24}h_{6}=h_{0}^{2}P^{6}d_{0}$
 and

$d_{6}h_{0}^{55}h_{7}=h_{0}^{2}x_{126,60}$
 in the Adams spectral sequence of 
$S^{0}$
 (from the image of 
$J$
), and 
$d_{3}v_{2}^{16}=\beta^{5}g$
 in the Adams spectral sequence of 
$\mathit{tmf}$
, derived from power operations (Bruner–Rognes 
[ 2 ]
).

Notation 3.5
.




In the table of Adams spectral sequence, columns 
stem,s
 refers to the degrees of the elements in the 
base
 column. The columns 
base,diff
 should be parsed as linear combinations of the monomial basis in the corresponding bidegree. If 
level=r<5000
, the 
base
 column is hit by a 
$d_{r}$
-differential from 
diff
. If 
level=10000-r>5000
, the 
base
 column supports a 
$d_{r}$
-differential hitting 
diff
. If 
level=9000
, it means that 
base
 is a permanent cycle. If 
diff
 is filled with 
[NULL]
, it means that the value of 
diff
 is undetermined. The reader can see that we assume that all visible nontrivial differentials in the data are of length shorter than 1000.








Notation 3.6
.




In the table of extension spectral sequence corresponding to a cofiber sequence










$X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z,$







the column 
iC=0,1,2
 refers to 
X,Y,Z
 where 
base
 lives respectively. The other columns are similar to those in Notation 
3.5
. If 
iC=1
 and 
level=r<5000
, then 
base
 is hit by an 
$f$
-extension which jumps filtration by 
$r$
. If 
iC=1
 and 
level=10000-r>5000
, then 
base
 supports an 
$g$
-extension which jumps filtration by 
$r$
.
