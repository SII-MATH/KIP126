Notation 2.1
.




In the table of generators, a row with 
$id=i$
 is the 
$i$
’th generator of the 
$E_{2}$
-page of 
X
. In this section we will denote this generator by 
$x_{i}$
 if 
X
 is a ring spectrum or 
$v_{i}$
 if 
X
 is a module over some ring spectrum (in our dataset there are only two ring spectra 
S0
 and 
tmf
). The columns 
stem,s
 are the degrees of the generators. The column 
name
 is the latex name we give to the 
$i$
’th generator. However, currently not all generators have latex names. The column 
name
 might be sometimes vacant (filled with 
[NULL]
).








Notation 2.2
.




In the table of relations, consider the column 
rel
 for relations. If 
X
 is a ring spectrum, then 
0,1;1,2,5,4
 denotes the relation










$x_{0}^{1}+x_{1}^{2}x_{5}^{4}=0.$







If 
X
 is a module over the ring spectra 
R
, then 
0,1,3;1,2,5,4,7
 denotes the relation










$x_{0}^{1}v_{3}+x_{1}^{2}x_{5}^{4}v_{7}=0$







where 
$v_{i}$
 is the 
$i$
’th generator of 
X
 and 
$x_{j}$
 is the 
$j$
’th generator of the ring spectrum 
R
.
In summary, the semicolon can be parsed as the plus sign and the monomials are sequences of sub- and upper-scripts whose lengths are even if 
X
 is a ring or odd if 
X
 is a module. The columns 
stem,s
 are the degrees of the relations which can be determined by the degrees of the generators.








Notation 2.3
.




In the table of basis, the column 
mon
 is parsed in the same way as we parse 
rel
 for relations. The only difference is that each 
mon
 is a monomial so there should be no semicolon in the column 
mon
. The columns 
stem,s
 are the degrees of the monomials. The column 
index
 is 
important
. When there are multiple monomial basis elements in the same bidegree, they will be indexed by 
$0,1,\dots,k$
. We write










$[i]\in\mathrm{Ext}^{\verb|s,stem+s|}(\verb|X|)$







to denote the monomial basis element with index 
$i$
 in 
$\mathrm{Ext}^{\verb|s,stem+s|}(\verb|X|)$
. In the Table of Proofs (see Section 
4
), the element will be written as










X (stem, s) [i]







in the 
info
 column. We can also write 
$[i_{1},i_{2},...]$
 to denote the linear combination of monomials with indices 
$i_{1},i_{2},\dots$
 and 
$[]$
 to denote the zero element. The 
d2
 column represents the 
$d_{2}$
 differentials of the basis elements in the format 
$[i_{1},i_{2},...]$
 we just described but in degree 
(stem-1,s+2)
. If it is empty, it means that the 
$d_{2}$
 differential is trivial. If it is filled with 
[NULL]
, it means that the 
$d_{2}$
 differential is not calculated yet by the program 
./Adams
 using the secondary Steenrod operations.

Notation 2.4
.




Let 
a,b,c,...
 be keywords, 
$a,b,c,\dots$
 be the corresponding elements in 
$\pi_{*}(S^{0})$
 and 
$|a|,|b|,|c|$
 be the topological degrees of 
$a,b,c,\dots$
. The following examples demonstrate our naming for the CW spectra.



 •
 



S0
 is the sphere spectrum $S^{0}$ . tmf is the topological modular form $tmf$ .

 
 •
 



Ca
 is the cofiber of 
$a$
.

 
 •
 



CW_a_b
 is a three-cell complex such that the following two cofiber sequences exist.










$\texttt{Ca}\to\texttt{CW\_a\_b}\to\Sigma^{|a|+|b|+2}\verb|S0|$















$\verb|S0|\to\texttt{CW\_a\_b}\to\Sigma^{|a|+1}\texttt{Cb}$







The existence of such complex corresponds to 
$a\cdot b=0$
 in 
$\pi_{*}(S^{0})$
.

 
 •
 



CW_a_b_c
 is a four-cell complex such that the following two cofiber sequences exist.










$\texttt{CW\_a\_b}\to\texttt{CW\_a\_b\_c}\to\Sigma^{|a|+|b|+|c|+3}\verb|S0|$















$\texttt{Ca}\to\texttt{CW\_a\_b\_c}\to\Sigma^{|a|+|b|+2}\texttt{Cc}$







The existence of such complex corresponds to 
$0\in\langle a,b,c\rangle$
 in 
$\pi_{*}(S^{0})$
.

 
 •
 



Inductively we can define 
CW_a_..._z
 as long as it exists.

 
 •
 



Ca_Cb
 is the smash product of 
Ca
 and 
Cb
. tmf_X is the smash product of tmf and X .

 
 •
 



CW_a_V_b
 is a three-cell complex such that the following two cofiber sequences exist.










$\verb|Ca|\to\verb|CW_a_V_b|\xrightarrow{\text{top cell b}}\Sigma^{|b|+1}\verb|S0|$















$\verb|Cb|\to\verb|CW_a_V_b|\xrightarrow{\text{top cell a}}\Sigma^{|a|+1}\verb|S0|$






 
 •
 



DX
 is a $k$ -fold suspension of the Spanier–Whitehead dual of X such that the lowest dimension of the cells is $0$ .

 
 •
 



CW_a_A_b
 is equivalent to DCW_a_V_b .

 
 •
 



C2h4
 is the same as CW_2_sigmasq . C2h5 is the same as CW_2_theta4 . C2h6 is the same as CW_2_theta5 .

 
 •
 



CW_a_b_V_c
 is a four-cell complex such that the following two cofiber sequences exist.










$\verb|CW_a_b|\to\verb|CW_a_b_V_c|\to\Sigma^{|c|+1}\verb|S0|$















$\verb|Cc|\to\verb|CW_a_b_V_c|\to\Sigma^{|a|+1}\verb|Cb|$






 
 •
 



CW_a_b_c_Eq_d_e
 is a five-cell complex such that the following two cofiber sequences exist.










$\verb|CW_a_b_V_d|\to\verb|CW_a_b_c_Eq_d_e|\to\Sigma^{|a|+|b|+|c|+3}\verb|S0|$















$\verb|Ca|\to\verb|CW_a_b_c_Eq_d_e|\to\Sigma^{\min\{|a|+|b|+2,|d|+1\}}\verb|CW_c_A_e|$







The existence of such complex corresponds to 
$d\cdot e\in\langle a,b,c\rangle$
 in 
$\pi_{*}(S^{0})$
.

 
 •
 



Joker
 is equivalent to CW_2_eta_2_Eq_eta_eta .

 
 •
 



RPm_n
 is equivalent to the stunted real projective space ${\mathbb{R}}P_{m}^{n}$ for positive integers m,n .

 
 •
 



Fphi
 is the suspension of $F\phi$ the fiber of the Kahn-Priddy map $\phi:{\mathbb{R}}P_{1}^{\infty}\to S^{0}$ (or the cofiber of $\phi$ ).

 
 •
 



Fphik
 is the suspension of the $k$ -skeleton of $F\phi$ for an integers k .

 









Remark 2.5
.




All the CW spectra we considered are connective spectra. The current programs only work well with connective spectra.








Remark 2.6
.




If there are two or more CW spectra with different homotopy types that satisfy the same naming condition, then the name represents all choices and we do not assume which one we pick. The current important input of the program is the existence of these cofiber sequences and the ones derived from these using the properties of triangulated categories. We will make assumptions on homotopy classes in the future when a new feature of the program is complete and it will enable the 
./ss
 program to rigorously determine if there exists at least one homotopy class that satisfies multiple constraints.








All the CW spectra we consider are known to exist in the literature. One major example is the existence of 
$tmf$
 and its 15-skeleton










$\verb|CW_sigma_nu_eta_2|,$







and we can get many subquotients of 
$\verb|CW_sigma_nu_eta_2|$
 and duals of these such as 
$\verb|CW_2_eta_nu|$
. The existence of 
$\verb|C2h4|,\verb|C2h5|,\verb|C2h6|$
 is due to the strong Kervaire invariant problem for 
$\sigma^{2},\theta_{4},\theta_{5}$
 (See 
[ 1 ]
 
[ 9 ]
).

We explain our naming scheme for these maps.






Notation 2.8
.




Let 
X,Y,Z
 be names of CW spectra. The following examples demonstrate our naming for the maps between CW spectra.



 •
 



If 
X
 has strictly fewer cells than 
Y
 and there exists a unique 
$k$
 and a unique inclusion (up to homotopy) such that 
X
 is a subcomplex of 
$\Sigma^{k}$
Y
, then 
X__Y
 denotes this subcomplex map 
X
$\hookrightarrow\Sigma^{k}$
Y
.

 
 •
 



If 
X
 has strictly more cells than 
Y
 and there exists a unique 
$k$
 and a unique quotient such that 
$\Sigma^{k}$
Y
 is a quotient complex of 
X
, then 
X__Y
 denotes this quotient map 
X
$\to\Sigma^{k}$
Y
.

 
 •
 



If we have a cofiber sequence










$\verb|X|\to\Sigma^{m}\verb|Y|\to\Sigma^{n}\verb|Z|\to\Sigma\verb|X|$







whose first map is a subcomplex inclusion denoted by 
X__Y
 and whose second map is a quotient map denoted by 
Y__Z
, then the third boundary map is denoted by 
Z__Q_Y
.

 
 •
 



For key words 
a,b,c,d,e
, the map 
CW_d_e__CW_a_b_by_c
 is equivalent to










$\verb|CW_d_e__Q_CW_a_b_c_d_e|.$







Similarly, if we have a map with name 
X__Y_by_c
 it means that 
X
 has a unique bottom cell and 
Y
 has a unique top cell and the map attaches the bottom cell of 
X
 to the top cell of 
Y
 by 
c
. In other words, these two cells form a subquotient of the cofiber of the map which is equivalent to 
Cc
.

 
 •
 



For numbers 
n,l,p,q
 where 
$p<q=n-1<n<l$
, the map 
RPn_l__RPp_q
 is equivalent to 
RPn_l__Q_RPp_l
. In other words, the following three maps form a cofiber sequence.










$(\verb|RPp_q__RPp_l|,~\verb|RPp_l__RPn_l|,~\verb|RPn_l__RPp_q|)$






 
 •
 



In plain-text names, we use the letter 
m
 to denote the negative sign. For example,
