\chapter{External-input provenance audit}
\label{chap:provenance-audit}

Citation and artifact metadata are maintained in the single canonical
\texttt{docs/external-inputs.json} manifest. They are not Lean mathematical
types or proof obligations. This chapter describes the audit policy; its
records do not belong in the mathematical dependency graph.

\section{Mathematical inputs and their sources}

Lean literature fields contain the precise mathematical proposition, with
all hypotheses and the selected model explicit. Computation fields contain
the interpreted mathematical statement or its certification data. The
manifest records source identifiers, theorem/page/table locators, acquisition
status, artifact paths and SHA-256 digests, together with links to the Lean
declarations and Blueprint labels. There is no parallel Lean citation
registry or proposition-with-citation wrapper.

Source-result, computation, model-comparison and internal-application roles
are recorded separately. A transport or consequence of an external theorem
has its own proof obligation; retaining the original citation does not make
that consequence a theorem from the cited source. Unavailable primary text
and secondary locators must be recorded honestly.

The source checker validates artifact paths, statuses and digests. The input
checker checks declaration and Blueprint correspondence and interface coverage.
Neither check establishes that a source entails the corresponding Lean
statement. Chapter~\ref{chap:external-results} lists the 48 direct
literature proposition fields individually. Each has a distinct canonical
mathematical node with its precise hypotheses and a direct Lean field link.
The checker reads the active Blueprint inputs and rejects a shared summary
node, an inactive label, or a link to a different field. The fixed-model
bindings, foundational language, computation comparisons and Main's internal
applications have separate nodes and do not increase this field count.
Chapter~\ref{chap:computed-inputs} likewise lists 29 individual computation
result statements and four binding comparison statements. Nested proposition
records are expanded, while interpretation and coordinate data are excluded
from the numbered statement inventory. Each of these 33 entries has a distinct
canonical node, its actual Lean projection and its owning delivery-field link.
The checker also verifies this expansion against the current Lean structures;
it rejects a missing leaf, a wrapper counted as a statement, a duplicate node
or an incorrect parent-field link. The seven top-level result fields remain
structural coverage records, rather than seven numbered theorem entries.
This correspondence is not certification of source fidelity or proof completion.

\section{Unified construction and trust boundary}
\label{def:kip126-trust-boundary}

The single \texttt{Challenge2} witness has exactly two root fields: literature
and computation. Each delivery has bindings and results; computation depends
on the same literature sources, and its bindings hold the fixed program
presentation and coordinate comparisons. Main states the internal route
application after consuming that witness. The former Challenge1
sphere-completeness and strong-convergence obligation, together with
sphere-filtration separation, are stated by unfinished Def theorems.
The route model, shared mathematical background, and fixed implementation
remain defined in Def.

During development, Main consumes one direct \texttt{Challenge2} axiom.
Interface must construct this witness without using the consumer axiom.
There is no additional existence wrapper or choice step. At final acceptance
the development axiom must be replaced by the proved construction and all
relevant \texttt{sorry} dependencies discharged. The intentional final Main
Challenge placeholder is never proof-completion evidence.

\section{Unfinished obligations}

A missing source, an unfinished model comparison, and an uncertified
computation remain explicit obligations.
Successful parsing, hashing, metadata validation or compilation does not
complete them.
