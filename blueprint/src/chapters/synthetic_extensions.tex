\chapter{Synthetic extension spectral sequences}
\label{chap:synthetic-extension-ss}

This chapter defines weightwise extension spectral sequences, the basic $\lambda$--$\rho$--$\delta$ triangle, and coherent solution towers.  Comparison theorems are deferred to the internal-results layer.

\section{Weightwise extension spectral sequences}

% SOURCE: KIP126/Main/Axiom/Literature/MainPaper/main.tex:998-1007.
\begin{definition}[Synthetic extension spectral sequence]
  \label{def:synthetic-extension-ss}
  \uses{def:synthetic-adams-ss,def:f-extension-ss}
  \notready
  For a weight-preserving map $f:X\to Y$ of finite synthetic spectra equipped
  with convergence-and-detection witnesses for their synthetic Adams
  sequences, filter
  $0\to\pi_{*,*}X\to\pi_{*,*}Y\to0$ by Adams filtration.  The resulting
  $f$-ESS has
  \[
    {}^fE_0^{s,t,w}\cong E_\infty^{s,t,w}(X)\oplus
    E_\infty^{s,t,w}(Y),
    \qquad d_n^f:(s,t,w)\mapsto(s+n,t+n,w).
  \]
\end{definition}

% SOURCE: KIP126/Main/Axiom/Literature/MainPaper/main.tex:999-1007, assertion that all Section 2 results remain valid.
\begin{proposition}[Weightwise transport of the ESS calculus]
  \label{prop:synthetic-ess-weightwise-transport}
  \uses{def:synthetic-extension-ss,prop:fextension-iff-detected,prop:fextension-inessential-iff-higher,prop:no-crossing-iff-uniform-detection,thm:fess-commutative-square,prop:fess-exact-middle-boundary}
  \notready
  Every definition and theorem of Chapter~\ref{chap:extension-ss} holds in a
  fixed synthetic weight, provided every map and comparison preserves that
  weight.  In particular, essentiality, crossings, the representative
  criterion, square propagation, and exact-middle boundary detection retain
  their statements with a third grading coordinate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Fix $w$ and regard the bigraded homotopy groups in that weight as filtered
  graded groups.  Reuse the two-term filtered-complex construction and each
  proof from Chapter~\ref{chap:extension-ss}; all maps preserve $w$, so no
  additional reindexing occurs.  Reassemble the family over all weights.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Strict filtered representative fibers]
  \label{def:strict-ess-representative-fiber}
  \uses{def:core-filtered-chain-complex,def:fess-cycles-boundaries}
  \lean{KIP126.Core.SpectralSequence.FilteredComplex.Solutions.equation,KIP126.Core.SpectralSequence.FilteredComplex.Solutions.Fiber,KIP126.Core.SpectralSequence.FilteredComplex.Solutions.differences,KIP126.Core.SpectralSequence.FilteredComplex.Solutions.coordinateEquiv}
  \leanok
  For a filtered complex $C$, a test object $T$, and integers $n,s,k$,
  take pairs of actual morphisms
  $a:T\to F^sC_k$ and $b:T\to F^{s+n}C_{k-1}$.
  Their equation map is the additive homomorphism
  \[
    \Phi(a,b)=(\pi_s a,\pi_{s+n}b,d\iota_s a-\iota_{s+n}b).
  \]
  The strict fiber with labels $(x,y)$ is
  $\Phi^{-1}(x,y,0)$, and its difference group is $\ker\Phi$.
  These are actual representative pairs, without a further quotient of the
  solution set.  Translation and subtraction give a free transitive action
  on every nonempty fiber; a chosen point identifies that fiber with the
  kernel.  This makes no nonemptiness assertion.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Strictification of an internal page relation]
  \label{prop:strict-ess-fiber-page-relation}
  \uses{def:strict-ess-representative-fiber,thm:filtered-complex-to-spectral-sequence,def:page-extension,def:filtered-chain-map}
  \lean{KIP126.Core.SpectralSequence.FilteredComplex.Solutions.differentialRelation_of_fiber,KIP126.Core.SpectralSequence.FilteredComplex.Solutions.differentialRelation_iff_nonempty_fiber,KIP126.Synthetic.PageExtension.finitePageExtension_iff_solutions,KIP126.Synthetic.PageExtension.infinitePageExtension_iff_solutions,KIP126.Synthetic.PageExtension.mem_targetCoset_iff_differentialRelation,KIP126.Synthetic.PageExtension.FiniteExtensionWitness.mem_targetCoset_iff_solutions,KIP126.Synthetic.PageExtension.InfiniteExtensionWitness.mem_targetCoset_iff_solutions,KIP126.Interface.Solution.finitePageExtension_restrict}
  \leanok
  Suppose the filtered complex is bounded and $n\ge0$.
  A strict solution gives the canonical internal relation $d_n(x)=y$.
  If $T$ is projective, the converse holds as well.
  In particular this applies to the free rank-one test module
  $\operatorname{ULift}(\mathbb Z)$ used for the actual synthetic ESS.
  More precisely, fix one normalized page family $P$, including its actual
  quotient maps, ESS and comparison maps.  For $r\ge2$, $n\ge e(f)$ and
  $n-e(f)<r-1$, fix
  \[
    x\in Z_{r-1}^{s,t}(X),\qquad
    y\in Z_{r-1-n+e(f)}^{s+n,t+n}(Y).
  \]
  The $(f,E_r)$-extension from $x$ to $y$ is equivalent to nonemptiness of
  $P$'s strict solution fiber at quotient $r-1$, using those same source
  and target labels and the actual $\lambda^{n-e(f)}$ target map.
  For permanent representatives $x,y$ and $n\ge e(f)$, the analogous
  equivalence holds for $P$'s untruncated ESS.

  Moreover, suppose $P$ is equipped with the filtration-preservation and
  label-comparison witnesses for its actual $\rho$ square.  For
  $0<i\le j$ and $n-e(f)<i$, a fixed-label extension on page $j+1$
  restricts to one on page $i+1$, provided the two chosen source labels
  and the two target labels are respectively equal as elements of $E_2$.
  These statements bind the fixed classical labels to the actual fibers.

  For any given finite or untruncated extension witness $W$, its complete
  actual target coset is also characterized by these fibers:
  \[
    z\in\operatorname{TargetCoset}(W)
      \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \operatorname{Sol}_P^n(x_W,z)\ne\varnothing.
  \]
  Here $z$ ranges over the target cycle subgroup displayed above (over
  permanent representatives in the untruncated case), $x_W$ is the same
  source representative as in $W$, and the coset is pulled back along
  $P$'s actual $\lambda$ target map from the ESS boundary coset of $W$.
  A single fixed-label fiber remains a set of representative pairs; the
  coset is the set of labels indexing its nonempty fibers.  These claims
  do not assert the existence of the comparison witnesses, restriction
  surjectivity, or a coherent-tower comparison.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Projectivity lifts a cycle through its actual cycle-kernel image.
  The discrepancy between its strict target and $y$ is an actual $B_n$
  boundary.  Lift that boundary through the differential image and subtract
  its primitive in $F^{s+1}C_k$ from the source lift.  This preserves $x$
  and makes the target equation strict.  Thus strictness does not discard
  the boundary ambiguity of the page relation.
  Specialize to the actual ESS and its free rank-one test module.  Unfold
  the fixed source and target comparisons to obtain the finite and
  untruncated page-extension equivalences.  The actual filtered $\rho$
  chain map sends strict solutions to strict solutions; the supplied label
  comparisons preserve the stated labels, giving finite restriction.
  Finally, subtract a representative of an actual ESS boundary to see that
  every target in the boundary coset satisfies the same page relation.
  The converse follows from equality on the page.  Pull this equivalence
  back along the fixed $\lambda$ target map and apply strictification.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Finite ESS solution torsors and coherent towers]
  \label{def:synthetic-ess-solution-tower}
  \uses{def:synthetic-extension-ss,def:strict-ess-representative-fiber,prop:strict-ess-fiber-page-relation,def:filtered-chain-map,def:synthetic-lambda-quotient}
  \lean{KIP126.Synthetic.PageExtension.NormalizedPageFamily.FiniteSolutions,KIP126.Synthetic.PageExtension.NormalizedPageFamily.InfiniteSolutions,KIP126.Synthetic.PageExtension.NormalizedPageFamily.PermanentFiniteSolutions,KIP126.Challenge2.PageExtensionRestrictionFiltration,KIP126.Challenge2.PageExtensionRestrictionLabels,KIP126.Challenge2.restrictFiniteSolution,KIP126.Challenge2.restrictPermanentFiniteSolution,KIP126.Challenge2.CoherentPageExtensionSolutions,KIP126.Core.SpectralSequence.FilteredComplex.Solutions.coordinateEquiv,KIP126.Core.SpectralSequence.FilteredComplex.Solutions.restrict,KIP126.Core.SpectralSequence.FilteredComplex.Solutions.restrict_translate}
  \leanok
  Fix one normalized page family $P$ for $f:X\to Y$, integers $n,s,t$
  with $n\ge e(f)$, and permanent representatives
  \[
    x\in Z_\infty^{s,t}(X),\qquad
    y\in Z_\infty^{s+n,t+n}(Y).
  \]
  Put $k=n-e(f)\ge0$.  For every natural-number quotient length $q>k$, let
  $x_q\in Z_q^{s,t}(X)$ and $y_q\in Z_{q-k}^{s+n,t+n}(Y)$ be the
  actual inclusions of these fixed permanent representatives.  Define
  $\operatorname{Sol}_q^n(x,y)$ to be the strict solution fiber of the
  actual two-term complex for $\widehat f_q$, with source label $x_q$
  and target label obtained from $y_q$ by the actual $\lambda^k$ map.
  All fibers use $P$'s fixed comparisons and synthetic degree
  $(t-s,t+e(f))$.  This is the type
  \texttt{PermanentFiniteSolutions}; it has precisely the pairs of
  Definition~\ref{def:strict-ess-representative-fiber}, with no further
  quotient.  A fixed-label fiber is not itself the set of possible target
  labels.

  Supply the filtration-preservation witness $I$ for $P$'s actual $\rho$
  squares and the label-comparison witness $J$ for that same $I$.
  When nonempty, each fiber is a torsor under its actual difference group
  $K_q=\ker\Phi_q$, and a chosen solution identifies the fiber with $K_q$.
  The filtered chain map supplied by the actual $\rho$ square and the two
  label comparisons give the restrictions
  \[
    \operatorname{res}_{q+1,q}:\operatorname{Sol}_{q+1}^n(x,y)
      \longrightarrow\operatorname{Sol}_q^n(x,y),\qquad q>k.
  \]
  At the level of actual representative pairs, these restrictions satisfy
  the generic equivariance with respect to the induced difference-group map.
  Define a \emph{coherent solution tower} to consist of a solution
  $u_q\in\operatorname{Sol}_q^n(x,y)$ for every $q>k$, together with
  the adjacent compatibility equations
  $\operatorname{res}_{q+1,q}(u_{q+1})=u_q$.  Thus its first quotient
  length is $m_0=k+1$.

  This defines the type of coherent solutions from the specified $P,I,J$.
  It neither constructs those comparison witnesses nor supplies a member
  of the type.  Restriction surjectivity and comparison with an actual
  untruncated solution or a homotopy limit remain separate obligations;
  nonemptiness of every finite fiber alone does not establish them.
\end{definition}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Use the filtered two-term complex defining the synthetic ESS.  A choice of
  filtered source lift and target lift is precisely a solution of its
  representative equation.  Subtracting two solutions gives the stated
  difference group, and addition acts freely and transitively.  The compatible
  quotient squares induce the restrictions, and the generic restriction
  theorem gives equivariance on representative pairs.  Include the fixed
  permanent labels into each finite cycle subgroup and collect the actual
  adjacent compatibility equations in the coherent-solution structure.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Coherence of quotient restrictions]
  \label{prop:lambda-quotient-restriction-coherence}
  \uses{def:synthetic-lambda-quotient,def:cofiber-triangle,def:lambda-rho-delta-triangle}
  \notready
  For every map of classical spectra $f:X\to Y$ in the stable homotopy
  context (so $\nu f:\nu X\to\nu Y$ is the induced synthetic map), put
  $Q_j(X):=\nu X/\lambda^j$ for each finite $j\ge1$ and write
  $\bar f_j:Q_j(X)\to Q_j(Y)$ for the map induced by $\nu f$.  For finite
  $1\le i\le j$, the quotient restriction maps are natural:
  \[
    \rho^Y_{i,j}\circ\bar f_j
      =\bar f_i\circ\rho^X_{i,j}.
  \]
  They satisfy the identity and composition laws
  \[
    \rho^X_{i,i}=\operatorname{id},\qquad
    \rho^X_{k,i}\circ\rho^X_{i,j}=\rho^X_{k,j}
    \quad(1\le k\le i\le j).
  \]
  For every finite pair $0<i<j$, the maps
  $\Sigma^{0,-i}\bar f_{j-i}$, $\bar f_j$, $\bar f_i$, and
  $\Sigma^{1,-i}\bar f_{j-i}$ form a morphism from the finite
  $\lambda$--$\rho$--$\delta$ triangle for $X$ at $(i,j)$ to the
  corresponding triangle for $Y$.  Thus multiplication, restriction, and
  connecting maps commute in the finite tower, naturally in $f$.  This
  proposition asserts no map of $E_\infty$- or $\mathrm{CAlg}$-objects; the
  $m=\infty$ endpoint is handled separately by the complete-tower statements.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Write $\mu^X_j:\Sigma^{0,-j}\nu X\to\nu X$ for multiplication by
  $\lambda^j$ (and put $\mu^X_0=\operatorname{id}_{\nu X}$).  For each finite
  $j$, the square
  \[
    \begin{array}{ccc}
      \Sigma^{0,-j}\nu X & \xrightarrow{\mu^X_j} & \nu X \\
      {\scriptstyle\Sigma^{0,-j}\nu f}\downarrow && \downarrow{\scriptstyle\nu f} \\
      \Sigma^{0,-j}\nu Y & \xrightarrow{\mu^Y_j} & \nu Y
    \end{array}
  \]
  commutes by naturality of multiplication by $\lambda^j$.  Applying the
  chosen cofiber construction gives $\bar f_j$.  For $i\le j$, define the shifted
  factor map
  \[
    q^X_{i,j}:=\Sigma^{0,-i}\mu^X_{j-i}:
      \Sigma^{0,-j}\nu X\longrightarrow\Sigma^{0,-i}\nu X,
    \qquad \mu^X_i\circ q^X_{i,j}=\mu^X_j.
  \]
  The square with horizontal maps $\mu^X_j,\mu^X_i$ and vertical maps
  $q^X_{i,j},\operatorname{id}_{\nu X}$ induces $\rho^X_{i,j}$ by the
  chosen cofiber construction.  The identities
  $q^X_{i,i}=\operatorname{id}$ and
  $q^X_{k,i}\circ q^X_{i,j}=q^X_{k,j}$ induce the displayed identity and
  composition laws.  Naturality of the $q^X_{i,j}$ squares with respect to
  $\nu f$, followed by the chosen triangle equivalences, gives the morphism
  of finite distinguished triangles with the four components displayed above.
  This is a finite cofiber calculation and uses no multiplicative algebra
  structure.
\end{proof}

\section{The elementary lambda and rho extensions}

\begin{definition}[Finite quotient-tower witness on the specified cofibers]
  \label{def:finite-lambda-quotient-witness}
  \lean{KIP126.Synthetic.Context.LambdaRhoDeltaTriangle,KIP126.Synthetic.Context.FiniteLambdaQuotientTower,KIP126.Synthetic.Context.FiniteLambdaQuotientTower.Hom,KIP126.Synthetic.FiniteQuotientTowerInterface}
  \uses{def:synthetic-lambda-quotient,def:cofiber-triangle}
  \leanok
  Every level of this witness is the already chosen cofiber $X/\lambda^n$.
  It supplies restrictions $\rho_{i,j}$ with identity and composition laws,
  compatibility with the actual quotient inclusions, and the finite
  $\lambda$--$\rho$--$\delta$ triangles. The first arrow in each triangle
  is also required to agree with the actual $\lambda^i$ on quotient
  inclusions. A map of witnesses uses precisely the cofiber maps induced
  by the original map $f$, with all three triangle squares specified.
  The root interface requests these witnesses naturally for the same
  family $\nu X$. It does not construct an inhabitant or a homotopy limit.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Naturality of the actual powers and finite quotient maps]
  \label{prop:finite-lambda-quotient-local-laws}
  \lean{KIP126.Synthetic.Context.lambdaPow_naturality,KIP126.Synthetic.Context.XModLambdaN.map,KIP126.Synthetic.Context.XModLambdaN.incl_naturality,KIP126.Synthetic.Context.XModLambdaN.proj_naturality,KIP126.Synthetic.Context.FiniteLambdaQuotientTower.lambda_comp_rho,KIP126.Synthetic.Context.FiniteLambdaQuotientTower.rho_comp_delta}
  \uses{def:finite-lambda-quotient-witness}
  \leanok
  The existing recursively defined $\lambda^n$ is natural. Its chosen
  cofiber maps commute with inclusion and boundary. In a supplied finite
  tower, consecutive arrows in each specified quotient triangle compose
  to zero. These facts do not provide the missing shift-action coherence,
  arbitrary power factorization, exactness of bigraded shifts, or identity
  and composition laws for the chosen cofiber maps; a construction of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:lambda-quotient-restriction-coherence} still needs
  those compatible data.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Express the same recursive powers as natural transformations and prove
  equality of their components with the original powers. Apply the chosen
  cofiber's inclusion and boundary squares. The final zero composites are
  the axioms of the displayed distinguished triangles.
  \leanok
\end{proof}

% SOURCE: KIP126/Main/Axiom/Literature/MainPaper/main.tex:1009-1021, Notation not:deltaandrho.
\begin{definition}[$\lambda$--$\rho$--$\delta$ quotient triangle]
  \label{def:lambda-rho-delta-triangle}
  \uses{def:synthetic-lambda-quotient,def:cofiber-triangle}
  \notready
  For $0<n<m\le\infty$, define the distinguished triangle
  \[
    \Sigma^{0,-n}\nu X/\lambda^{m-n}
      \xrightarrow{\lambda^n}\nu X/\lambda^m
      \xrightarrow{\rho_{n,m}}\nu X/\lambda^n
      \xrightarrow{\delta_{n,m}}
      \Sigma^{1,-n}\nu X/\lambda^{m-n}.
  \]
  For $m=\infty$, the first two terms are
  $\Sigma^{0,-n}\nu X$ and $\nu X$.  Translation in the synthetic category is
  suspension by $S^{1,0}$.
\end{definition}
